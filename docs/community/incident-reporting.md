# Handling a Code of Conduct report (internal guide)

This page is for the people at Umbaji who receive a report made under the
[Code of Conduct](../project/code-of-conduct.md). It is public on purpose: a
person who reports should know what will happen to what they tell us.

## Who receives a report

| Role | Who | Reached at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code of Conduct lead | Leroy Mataa-Hewa Abiguime | contact@umbaji.org |
| Deputy | Justin Essowe Bakoubolo | justin.bakoubolo@umbaji.org |

The lead handles the report. The deputy takes it over, alone, when the report
is about the lead, about someone the lead works with every day, or when the
lead is away for more than two days. A reporter may always write to the deputy
directly, and does not have to say why.

Whoever has a conflict of interest steps aside and says so; they are not
copied on anything that follows.

## The steps

1. **Acknowledge within 24 hours.** Thank the reporter, say who handles the
   report, and when they will hear back. Do not ask them to justify
   themselves, and do not contact the person reported yet.
2. **Record it.** One entry in the private incident log (a restricted folder
   that only the lead and the deputy can open, never a GitHub issue, never a
   shared chat): the date, the reporter, what was reported, the links or
   screenshots, and every later step. Personal data stays in that log and
   nowhere else.
3. **Contain, if needed.** When the behaviour is ongoing or someone is at risk,
   act first: hide or lock the comment, the issue or the discussion, or
   suspend access to the repository. Containment is not a decision on the
   facts.
4. **Look at the facts within 7 days.** Read the public record; speak with the
   reporter and, when it does not put the reporter at risk, with the person
   reported. Witnesses only with the reporter's agreement.
5. **Decide.** The lead and the deputy decide together (the deputy and another
   maintainer, when the lead stepped aside), using the four levels of the Code
   of Conduct: correction, warning, temporary ban, permanent ban.
6. **Tell both people.** The reporter learns what was decided; the person
   reported learns what they did, what follows, and how to appeal. The
   reporter's name is never given without their agreement.
7. **Close the entry** in the log, with the decision and its date.

## Appeals

The person reported may appeal once, in writing, within 14 days. The appeal is
read by a maintainer who took no part in the decision, who confirms or changes
it within 14 days. That answer is final.

## Confidentiality

- What is in a report is shared only with the people who handle it.
- Nothing about a report is published, except a decision that has to be seen
  to be enforced (a ban), and then without the reporter's name.
- The log is kept for three years after the entry is closed, then deleted.

## When it goes beyond the project

- **Someone is in danger**, or the report describes a crime: tell the
  reporter to contact the police or emergency services, and help them do so.
  The project does not investigate crimes.
- **The report is about an Umbaji employee at work**: it also follows
  the employer's own procedure.
- **The project's funders** are informed when a funding agreement requires
  it, without the names of the people involved unless the agreement demands
  them.
- **A security vulnerability** is not a conduct report: it follows
  [Security](../project/security.md).
